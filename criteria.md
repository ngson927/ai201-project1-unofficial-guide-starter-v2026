# Acceptance criteria — The Unofficial Guide

Five criteria that say what "working" means for this system, written in unit 1
**before** any results existed.

An acceptance criterion names a target: a number, a count, a rate, or something
a person could plainly observe. *"Retrieval works"* is an opinion. *"For at
least 4 of my 5 test questions, the top results include a chunk containing the
answer"* is a criterion.

Under each one, write a sentence or two on **why that target** and not a
stricter or looser one. A reason that says something about your corpus or your
pipeline earns credit; *"80% seemed reasonable"* does not.

> Missing your own targets next unit costs you nothing. Setting a target so
> easy you can't miss it does.

---

## 1. Retrieved chunks contain the answer

For at least 4 of my 5 test questions, the retrieved chunks include one that
contains the answer.

**Why this target:** Four of my five questions are answered by a single
labelled section, and after Milestone 3 each of those sections is its own
chunk carrying its town name, so I expect them to retrieve cleanly. The fifth
asks where the nearest full hospital is, and my corpus disagrees with itself
about that: nine documents repeat a boilerplate block naming Brightwater and
only `guide_accessibility.md` names Marchwood, which is the correct answer.
With nine near-identical chunks competing against one, I do not expect
retrieval to surface the right chunk, so I am setting the target at four
rather than five and naming in advance which one I think will fail.

---

## 2. Every answer names a source

Every answer the system produces names at least one source document.

**Why this target:** All five and not four, because this is the one criterion
where a miss means my code is broken rather than my tuning being off. I am
counting the citation the model writes inside the answer, not the
`Sources retrieved:` line that `app.py` prints — that line is generated by my
own code from the retrieval results, so it is always present and counting it
would make this criterion impossible to fail. The in-answer citation is the
model's job: `build_prompt` ends with "name the file you used", and every
chunk is labelled `[from <filename>]` in the prompt. So the file name is
always available to the model. If an answer still comes back without one, the
instruction is being ignored, and that is worth catching.

---

## 3. The relevance gate stops out-of-corpus questions

When I ask a question my documents clearly don't cover, the relevance gate
stops it and the system returns "I don't have enough information about that" —
in at least 4 of 5 tries.

     just keep five of them, or the "4 of 5" above has nothing to be 4 of. -->

**Why this target:** I have not tuned the cutoff yet — that is Milestone 4 —
but the three distances I have seen so far sit in two clearly separated
groups. Two in-corpus questions came back at 0.402 and 0.416, and the
starter's sample question about a housing lottery, which belongs to a
different corpus entirely, came back at 0.773 and was refused. With a gap
that wide around the default cutoff of 0.6, I expect the five `OUT_OF_SCOPE`
questions to be refused comfortably. I am still saying four of five rather
than five of five because all three of those numbers came from a sample of
three, and a question that happens to share vocabulary with my documents —
"engine", say, against guides full of transport sections — could land closer
than the gap suggests.

---

## 4. Chunks stand on their own

For at least 4 of 5 chunks sampled with `python app.py chunks -n 5`, I can
state a specific question that chunk answers using only the text inside it,
without reading the chunk before or after it.

**Why this target:** Four of five and not five of five because I already know
one kind of chunk fails this. My chunker splits on `##` headings, and the text
that sits above the first heading — the opening paragraph of a guide — becomes
a chunk with a title but no section. The one in my README reads "An honest
assessment rather than a promotional one", which tells you a guide exists and
nothing else. There are 14 documents and so at most 14 of these among 94
chunks, which is roughly one in seven, so a five-chunk sample will usually
contain one and occasionally two.

I picked this over the two other things I measured, because both of those I
already know pass: 0 of 94 chunks start mid-sentence and 94 of 94 carry their
document title. Setting either as a target would be setting something I cannot
miss. This one I have not measured, and judging it means reading the chunk and
deciding, which is the part I am least sure I can do consistently — if I score
the same chunk differently twice, that is a finding about the criterion rather
than about the chunker.



---

## 5. Contradictions are surfaced, not resolved silently

When I ask the hospital question, whose answer my documents disagree about,
the system must either name more than one source document or refuse. It must
not state a single location as settled fact with one citation.

**Why this target:** This is the failure I care most about, because it is the
one a user cannot detect. A wrong answer that cites a real file, in the
confident voice the rest of my answers use, looks exactly like a right one.

The disagreement is specific and I found it by reading, before running
anything. Nine of my fourteen documents end with an identical "Practical
notes" block saying the nearest full hospital is in Brightwater.
`guide_accessibility.md` says it is in Marchwood, and that one is correct —
the boilerplate is copy-paste that was never updated, which you can see from
the fact that `guide_brightwater.md` tells you the nearest hospital is in
Brightwater and `guide_marchwood.md`, the town that actually has it, says the
same.

I am not asking for the right answer here, which is why this is not simply a
harder version of criterion 1. `TOP_K` is 5 and nine chunks carry the wrong
claim, so the correct chunk may never be retrieved at all and no prompt
wording would rescue that. What I am asking is that the system not sound
certain when its own sources conflict. I expect to miss this one: nothing in
my pipeline currently compares retrieved chunks against each other, so the
likely outcome is a confident answer naming Brightwater, and that is the
concrete thing I will have to diagnose and fix.
